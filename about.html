I am Emir Cabalak. I build machine learning models and then try to break my own results before anyone else does.

Most of my work so far is on search and analytics data at FlyRank, where I spend more time on how a model is evaluated than on which model it is. I am the person on a team who asks where the split came from.

The three cases on this site are the same story told three ways: a number that looked good was good for the wrong reason, and finding out cost me the number.

How I work

These are not aspirations. Each one is a rule I follow because breaking it cost me something specific, and each one shows up in at least one of the cases.

  • Leakage is a property of the time window, not a list of column names. A blacklist protects you from the columns you already caught.
  • Grouped splits when the data has entities, time-aware splits when it has a time axis, and both when it has both.
  • Publish the flattering number next to the honest one. The gap is a finding.
  • Every metric is quoted from the run that produced it. Never recomputed on a refit that trained on the rows it is scoring.
  • A precision-style figure without its base rate next to it is not a figure.
  • Disclose the population filter. How the rows were chosen is part of the result.
  • Say what the work does not show. Every case here has a section for that.
